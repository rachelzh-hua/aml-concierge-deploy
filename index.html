Transcriptomic Reanalysis of Polyamine Depletion in AML Leukemia Stem Cells Reveals SMOX mRNA–Protein Divergence and LSC-Selective Pathway Suppression

Abstract

Rondeau et al. (2024) demonstrated that polyamine depletion via DENSpm selectively targets AML leukemia stem cells (LSCs) by reducing eIF5A hypusination and KAT7 protein expression. We reanalyze the RNA-seq dataset (GEO GSE274658) using DESeq2 [6] with a patient-paired design and fgsea [7] GSEA. DENSpm induces 999 DEGs in LSCs but only 19 in HSPCs. SAT1 upregulation (log₂FC = +1.53, padj = 0.017) and KAT7 mRNA stability (log₂FC = −0.04, padj = 0.87) are confirmed. We identify SMOX as the genome-wide most significant DEG (log₂FC = +1.88, padj = 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁶) — yet the paper reported no SMOX protein change [1], revealing an mRNA–protein divergence consistent with the eIF5A/hypusination model.

1. Introduction

AML is driven by leukemia stem cells (LSCs) that persist through treatment and drive relapse [2]. Rondeau et al. (2024) identified spermidine as the most enriched metabolite in LSCs vs. HSPCs and showed polyamine depletion via DENSpm targets LSCs by reducing eIF5A hypusination [3] and KAT7 protein expression [4][5]. KAT7 regulation is post-transcriptional — mRNA levels are unchanged [1].

We reanalyze the RNA-seq data (GSE274658) with DESeq2 [6], perform GSEA with fgsea [7] and MSigDB [8], and compare with 9 testable paper claims.

2. Methods

Dataset: GSE274658 — 60,708 genes × 16 samples. 5 AML patients (ROS-low LSCs) + 3 normal BM donors (CD34⁺ HSPCs), each ± DENSpm 10 µM 24h. NovaSeq 6000, STAR v2.7.9a → GENCODE v38, featureCounts v2.0.1.

DESeq2: Paired design (~patient + treatment). LSC: n=5 paired; HSPC: n=3 paired. LSC-selective: padj < 0.05 in LSC, padj > 0.1 in HSPC.

GSEA: fgsea v1.32.4 [7] on Wald statistics, MSigDB Hallmark + KEGG [8].

3.3 SMOX: #1 DEG with mRNA–protein divergence

SMOX is the most significant DEG genome-wide (log₂FC = +1.88, padj = 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁶). It catalyzes spermine → spermidine, representing a compensatory response. The paper found no SMOX protein change [1], creating mRNA–protein divergence consistent with eIF5A-dependent post-transcriptional silencing [3]. This response is LSC-selective (HSPC: padj = 0.61, NS).

4. Discussion

8/9 paper claims confirmed. SMOX mRNA–protein divergence suggests compensatory transcription silenced by reduced eIF5A hypusination — a "futile response" paralleling KAT7 [1][4]. SMOX inhibition combined with DENSpm could enhance LSC targeting.

5. Limitations

  1. Computational reanalysis only — no new experimental data.
  2. Small sample size (n=5 AML, n=3 normal).
  3. No formal interaction model (patient nested in cell type).
  4. SMOX mRNA–protein divergence inferred, not independently verified.
  5. Single time point (24h).

6. Conclusion

Independent DESeq2 reanalysis confirms 8/9 claims from Rondeau et al. (2024). SMOX as the #1 DEG — with mRNA upregulation but no protein change — extends the eIF5A/hypusination model and generates the testable hypothesis that SMOX inhibition enhances DENSpm efficacy in AML.
